Why this session exists

"We are halfway to Week 2. By Meet 4 you will write Solidity. But Solidity is a means to an end. If your answer to 'what does it do' is 'it's a staking protocol,' you have built nothing. Staking is a primitive. It is the verb. What is the noun?"

Block A · 30 min

EDIPT, the Web3 dialect

For each phase: the standard definition, then the Web3 twist.

Empathize

Standard: observe a real user; understand their context, frustrations, workarounds.

Web3 twist: your user has additional friction layers — wallet creation, seed phrases, gas, RPC errors, mental models of "approval" vs "transfer".

Bandung specifics: candidate users typically already own a wallet (Coinbase Wallet or MetaMask) via the e-commerce/crypto wave, but may have zero DeFi experience. Optimize for their first dApp interaction being yours.

Define

Standard: synthesize empathy data into a problem statement of the form:

"<user> needs <need> because <insight>."

Web3 twist: add an explicit clause:

"…and the trust assumption that makes a smart-contract solution materially better than a centralized one is <X>."

If you can't fill in <X>, you don't have a Web3 problem; you have a regular SaaS problem with extra steps.

Ideate

Standard: divergent thinking. Sketch lots of solutions, judge later.

Web3 twist: every idea must answer four questions before it's even considered:

  1. Who pays gas? (Sponsored? Subsidized? User?)
  2. Who custodies value? (Self-custody? Smart-wallet abstraction? Treasury?)
  3. What's on-chain vs off-chain? (Don't try to put everything on-chain.)
  4. Failure mode if the front-end disappears? (Can users still recover funds?)

Prototype

Standard: lowest fidelity that lets you test the riskiest assumption.

Web3 twist: the riskiest assumption is almost always user behavior, not the smart contract. Prototype the user journey on paper or in Figma before you write Solidity.

Counter-intuitive: the contract is easier than the UX. We have the staking primitive done by Meet 4. The hard work is figuring out who wants to use it.

Test

Standard: put the prototype in front of real users; observe.

Web3 twist: test on testnet. Get 3–5 humans through your dApp on Arbitrum Sepolia before Demo Day. Watch them. Note where they get stuck.

More valuable than any code review.

Block B · 30 min

Empathize: identifying a real Bandung user

Small groups of 3. Brainstorm user archetypes specifically grounded in Bandung life. Examples to seed the conversation (don't give as answers):

The Tokopedia/Shopee small-seller who already accepts QRIS.
The Gojek/Grab driver who sends remittance home weekly.
The ITB campus organizer running a 200-person student event budget.
The freelance designer occasionally taking USDC on Twitter from foreign clients.
The boba-shop owner running a paper loyalty stamp card.
Push back when

A paragraph reads like "they want to earn passive income from crypto" — that's not Web3-empathy, that's wishful thinking. What does Tuesday afternoon look like for this person?

Block C · 30 min

Define + Ideate: 5 problems, 5 directions

Five concrete problem briefs. Each leaves design room — a "naive" framing fits a vanilla staking contract; an "ambitious" framing wraps the primitive in something users want.

Click any card to flip it. Front shows the user + workaround; back shows naive/ambitious framings + the trust insight.

BRIEF 1

Pasar Vendor Loyalty Pool

User: A wet-market vendor in Pasar Baru/Kosambi who accepts QRIS and has a Coinbase Wallet via their nephew.

Workaround: Paper loyalty cards. Hand-stamped. Lost in the rain.

click to flip →

BACK · Brief 1

Naive: Customers stake loyalty points; longer hold = higher discount.

Ambitious: Multi-vendor pool; stamps from any participating vendor fund shared marketing.

Trust: Vendors burned by Tokopedia fee changes; an open accumulator no central party can rewrite is materially better.

BRIEF 2

ITB Student Org Treasury

User: Treasurer of a 200-member ITB student org. Manages dues, event budgets, yearly handover.

Workaround: Shared GoPay/BCA account in one student's name, screenshotted balances.

click to flip →

BACK · Brief 2

Naive: Members stake annual USDC dues; earn org governance token; spending requires multi-sig.

Ambitious: The contract IS the org ledger. Handover becomes adding a signer — zero re-key work.

Trust: "One student holds the account" breaks at graduation; multi-sig removes the SPOF.

BRIEF 3

Coffee Roastery Co-op

User: Owner of a small-batch roastery near Dago, co-buying green beans with 4 other roasters.

Workaround: WhatsApp group, screenshot transfers, one "treasurer roaster" books imports.

click to flip →

BACK · Brief 3

Naive: Roasters stake rupiah-pegged tokens per batch; contract releases on milestones.

Ambitious: Reusable small-business escrow module; batch cost history accrues as public negotiation leverage.

Trust: Treasurer's personal account creates tax exposure + disputes; on-chain co-op removes both.

BRIEF 4

Gig-Worker Rainy-Day Pool

User: Bandung Gojek/Grab driver. Variable daily income, worries about Ramadan slowdowns and motorbike repairs.

Workaround: Arisan — informal rotating savings with neighbors. Sometimes the pot-holder absconds.

click to flip →

BACK · Brief 4

Naive: N drivers stake equal USDC monthly; contract distributes pooled monthly amount on rotation.

Ambitious: Priority claim unlocks for emergencies; vote-based by 2 other members.

Trust: Arisan exists; the missing piece is settlement reliability. smart contract removes the human pot-holder.

BRIEF 5

Local Creator Patronage with Vesting

User: Bandung indie musician / illustrator / podcaster with 500–2,000 super-fans.

Workaround: Trakteer/Saweria/Patreon-equivalents that take 5–20% and can deplatform.

click to flip →

BACK · Brief 5

Naive: Fans stake USDC; creator earns a continuous stream. Fans can unsubscribe; already-streamed rewards are non-refundable.

Ambitious: Milestone stakes; funds unlock only on creator-minted milestone NFT.

Trust: Patreon's existence rests on "fans can't directly pay creators" — false at sub-cent gas. Platform fee is rent that no longer needs to exist.

Block D · 30 min

Prototype + Test (paper, then wallet)

D.1. Paper prototype · 15 min

  • Draw 3 screens on paper: landing, primary action, success.
  • Annotate: where does the wallet connect? Where does gas appear? What error states are you designing for?
  • Show your neighbor. Without explaining, see if they guess what your dApp does in 10 seconds.

D.2. Wallet walk-through · 15 min

Groups of 3. One plays "user", walks through their imagined flow using only their actual wallet. The other two watch silently. 5-minute debrief: where did the imaginary user get confused?

Insight

We just did a usability test with zero code. Notice the value-to-effort ratio. Do this with five real users in Week 4 — that's the bar before Demo Day.

Closing · 10 min

Commit your problem statement

Each attendee writes — on a sticky note or in the cohort chat — their committed problem statement for Meet 4. These are not permanent, but they have to exist by end of today, because Meet 4's contract is the engine underneath this product.

Ground rules for picking
  • You may pick from the 5 briefs OR propose your own.
  • You may not pick "vanilla staking pool with no consumer wrapper." That is not a product.
  • You must commit to one problem before Meet 4 starts.
